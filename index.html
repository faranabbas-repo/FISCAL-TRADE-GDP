Summary

Fiscal balance, trade openness, and GDP vary significantly across SAARC countries over the past two decades. This project explores how fiscal policy and trade patterns relate to economic output in Afghanistan, Bangladesh, Bhutan, India, Maldives, Nepal, Pakistan, and Sri Lanka from 2000 to 2024. Using data from the World Bank’s WDI API, I cleaned and analyzed three key indicators: GDP, fiscal balance, and trade as % of GDP. The main plot reveals a strong positive association between trade openness and GDP levels, particularly in more integrated economies like India and Bangladesh.

This project investigates how fiscal policy and international trade have influenced GDP growth across SAARC countries from 2000 to 2024. Using data from the World Bank’s World Development Indicators (WDI), it analyzes trends in trade openness, government revenue, and GDP to understand their relationships within the region. The findings highlight the economic dynamics shaping these South Asian economies over the past two decades.
